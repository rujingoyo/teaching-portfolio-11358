Hello, and welcome to my digital teaching portfolio. I will go through my Year 10 Mathematics resource, which teaches students to critically analyse an AI-generated solution. The resource is aligned with the Australian Curriculum, which emphasises factorising and simplifying algebraic expressions using mathematical laws and properties. However, I want students to both achieve the correct answers and evaluate the AI method through a discussion of correctness, purpose, explainability and efficiency.

In this question, students are asked to evaluate \(x+2y\) from a given equation. AI treated the question as a quadratic equation in \(x\) and used the discriminant to draw a connection to what is required. This solution produced the correct answer, yet the purpose is not to correct AI or teach students that AI is simply unreliable. Instead, students are encouraged to retain the ability to deploy their own reasoning. To achieve this, students examine the steps, verifying them and interpreting the purpose of the steps and how AI answered the question. Students are expected to question the output rather than trust it directly.

Students then compare the AI response with an alternative method. By suggesting factorising, students will observe the appearance of \(x+2y\). This factorised term connects to the question directly, in contrast to the AI method that uses the discriminant unintuitively. The purpose of comparing the two methods is to recognise structure, justify equivalence and select a strategy. This is supported by research that demonstrated a complementary relationship between conceptual and procedural fluency. It is also a self-explanation activity with worked examples, which has been found effective in another study.

In addition to the mathematical learning goal, safety and human agency are also explicit. Students must follow school rules, including which AI tools or AI resources are permitted and which information should never be put into AI. Transparency is also endorsed. Students are required to record the model, prompts and the way it is used. This expectation also aligns with the Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools. The key message in this activity is about letting students, not AI, be responsible for verification, explanation, learning and the final decision.

This teaching resource also supports wellbeing. As students’ maths anxiety and performance are often negatively correlated, the activity aims to reduce the demand for external responses while encouraging individual thinking and preserving the challenges.

Finally, the resource addresses all three Unit Learning Outcomes, including safe, responsible and transparent AI use, a curriculum-aligned digital learning activity that develops mathematical reasoning, and structured prompts, accessible presentation and multiple ways for participation.
