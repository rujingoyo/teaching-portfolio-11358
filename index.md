I am a pre-service secondary teacher specialising in Mathematics and Computing and currently completing a Master of Secondary Teaching at the University of Canberra. My practice includes curriculum-aligned planning, differentiated instruction, formative assessment and the creation of positive learning environments.

My teaching philosophy is that conceptual understanding and procedural fluency should develop together. Procedures provide efficiency, yet relying solely on procedures can neither connect the concept to the application nor enable students to adapt their skills in unconventional scenarios. Rittle-Johnson and Schneider (2014) found a positive relationship between conceptual understanding and procedural fluency, which complement each other. Therefore, a meaningful teaching activity invites students to observe mathematical expression structures, analyse and compare different solutions, discover the conceptual components that lie within them and therefore refine their problem-solving skills.

Wellbeing in mathematical learning is highly relevant, as maths anxiety and performance are closely correlated (Carey et al., 2016). The environment I aim to create is a safe place where students can express confusion, engage in trial and error and seek help while maintaining cognitive challenges.

This teaching resource asks students to critically analyse AI’s approach to a problem by verifying, interpreting and comparing the methods. AI will act as a material that promotes critical thinking and mathematical reasoning, rather than an agent for replacement.
