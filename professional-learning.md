## Pedagogical purpose

I chose a teaching resource that includes a correct, yet less structurally direct AI response over an incorrect hallucination. This design guides students to think in terms of clarity, efficiency and logic, rather than teaching students that the AI solution is incorrect.

Bulut and Borromeo Ferri (2026) pointed out that students still struggle to justify or verify ChatGPT-supported mathematical modelling and exhibit uncritical acceptance. This teaching resource, therefore, sets up four dimensions: correctness, purpose, explainability and efficiency to help students analyse AI’s mathematical reasoning.

While the solution is correct, a more efficient and intuitive solution exists that uses factorising to establish a direct connection from the process (i.e. expression in terms of image) to the goal. Comparing two methods requires reasoning beyond correctness; it must also include interpretation of different conceptual understandings forming different procedural fluencies. This reflects the iterative relationship between conceptual and procedural knowledge (Rittle-Johnson & Schneider, 2014). The resource promotes self-explanation, which can further improve conceptual understanding algebraically (Booth et al., 2013). AI acts as material that encourages reasoning rather than answer authority.

## Wellbeing, inclusion and multimodal design

This activity provides psychological safety as students aim to analyse teacher-provided answers. Students are not required to share their own solutions to avoid the pressure or worry generated by uncertainty or potential mistakes, and instead, this creates a safe environment in which to participate. The four different dimensions provide various entry points. Some may commence with verifying correctness, some can interpret purposes and expressions, whereas others can initiate a discussion on efficiency. This maintains both the cognitive challenges in Year 10 and the consideration of performance and maths anxiety (Carey et al., 2016). In addition, teacher-supplied resources also allow full participation without requiring any student accounts.

## Safety, ethics and human agency

During the use of AI, this resource emphasises safety, transparency and responsibility. Students are required to use school- or teacher-approved AI and record information for transparency. These choices enact the Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools principles of transparency, accountability, critical thinking, privacy and human responsibility (Department of Education, 2023). Students, not AI, are responsible for the use of AI, including verification, explanation and selection.

## Unit Learning Outcomes

ULO1 is demonstrated through protecting privacy and verifying AI-generated content for responsibility, safety and ethics.

ULO2 is demonstrated through a curriculum-aligned activity addressing AC9M10A01 (ACARA, n.d.), in which students use algebra to construct mathematical reasoning.

ULO3 is demonstrated through accessible multimodal design, structured engagement and a psychologically safer entry into mathematical dialogue.
